Strip out the World Cup and the entire year spans 81.8 in January to 110.8 in September. That is a flat category — a genuinely seasonal one runs a 150–200 point spread. The largest mover of search interest here is not a season at all. It is a tournament that happens every four years.

Google Trends reports a relative interest index (0–100), not search volume. Every figure on this page is an index. Nothing here is a unit forecast.

Verdicts on assumed peaks

Busted

“Summer — June and July — is peak soccer ball season.”

The most intuitive belief about this category, and it is false. In ordinary years June indexes 103.6 and is elevated in only 4 of 17 years. July indexes 88.1 and has been elevated in 0 of 17 — it is one of the two worst months of the year.

The apparent summer peak is entirely tournament contamination: June reads 153.4 in World Cup years against 103.6 in ordinary ones, a 49.7-point gap. Pool all years together and June misleadingly becomes the top month at 121.2.

Women’s World Cup years (2007, 2011, 2015, 2019, 2023) sit inside the ordinary-year baseline above and still produce no June peak. The men’s tournament is the only mover.

Confirmed

“There is a back-to-school and fall-season bump.”

The one real, plannable pattern in the data. September indexes 110.8 and is elevated in 13 of 17 ordinary years — the only month clearing the consistency bar. The climb into it is the year’s largest ordinary move: August +9.9, then September +12.8 month-over-month.

The weekly file corroborates it independently: September 2025 averaged 36.0 against October at 33.5, in a stretch with no tournament activity.

The honest limit: September is the highest month on average and the most consistently elevated, but it is the outright best month in only 4 of 17 years. This is a reliable shallow bump, not a spike. Size the spend to match.

Busted

“December holiday gifting drives a peak.”

December indexes 98.8 — below average — and is elevated in 2 of 17 ordinary years. November is marginally better at 106.1 but still fails consistency at 35%. The December 2022 reading of 145.2 that would seem to support gifting was Qatar, not Christmas.

These are the three beliefs most commonly held about the category, standing in for assumptions that were not supplied — including the one the relatedQueries export shows the market actively acting on. Swap in real assumptions and the verdicts can be re-run.

Tournament overlay

You are here

The 2026 World Cup peaked the week of 2026-06-14 at 100. The most recent observation, 2026-08-09, reads 43 — down 57% from that peak. The tournament window is closed and interest is reverting to baseline.

The next men’s World Cup is 2030. The next three and a half years are ordinary years. Run the baseline calendar and do not budget for another June.

Qatar is why this cannot be handled with a standard seasonal adjustment. The contamination is not even at a fixed point in the calendar, so the tournament windows have to be identified and excluded deliberately.

For 2030, the pattern across five tournaments is consistent enough to plan against: interest climbs roughly 4–6 weeks before kickoff, peaks during the group stage — in 2026 the week of June 14, the tournament’s first week — and collapses within 2–3 weeks of the final. Apply the standard lead times to those dates once confirmed, and confirm the actual window rather than assuming June.

Caveats

  1. Region is Worldwide, not a single market. The geoMap export ranks South Africa (100) and Australia (84) above the United States (66) — opposite hemispheres. September is autumn in the north and spring in the south, so both plausibly drive a September bump. The timing is corroborated; the mechanism is not established. A US-only export would settle it and could shift the ramp by weeks.
  2. Weekly ramp timing is inferred from monthly data. The 22-year file is monthly, so a true ramp-start week cannot be computed from it — the analyzer’s week-level output for that file is an artifact and was discarded. The August ramp start is derived from month-over-month change. The only weekly file available covers a single, tournament-contaminated year.
  3. Long-run decline is search behaviour, not market size. The raw annual mean fell from 63.9 in 2004 to 17.6 in 2020 — a 72% drop — recovering to 25.7 in 2025. This reflects migration to Amazon and retailer apps. Do not read it as collapsing demand.
  4. Term choice. The relatedQueries export ranks the singular soccer ball at 99 against this plural term. The seasonal shape should be similar, but the plural is the smaller variant.
